
I thought of an analogy which describes my experience using my computer. Turning it on is like strapping myself to the side of a haywire missile. There is a map of targets, and far away lies the land of productivity. But often, overcome by the power and instability of the vehicles force, I teeter, spin and spiral off. Often I crash into that big red landmass, so hard to miss, YouTube. Other times I'm found scrolling on Hacker News, discord or Email (of all things). Why does this vehicle feel so unstable to operate?

I won't go into the psychology of *why* I believe distraction occurs. While making a conscious habit of tracking my distracting thoughts has been worthwhile, I like to approach the problem empirically. No questions why, just the find the hacks which produce results.
### Things That Work For Me
Below are three techniques that have been effective in narrowing my attention while on my computer.
#### PluckEye
[This](https://pluckyfilter.com/) program enables adding programmable time-restrictions to websites. Additive changes are applied instantly while subtraction changes are subject to a time-delay. I've used it for about two years now. While it has some rough edges, the core mechanics work great. In general, I've found that I grow more respect for tools which I spend time configuring and personalizing. In this case, a polished configuration prevents me from circumventing it's rules. Below is a snippet.

```
action:block	host:youtube.com
when:MTWRF17-22	action:allow	host:youtube.com
```
#### Daily Planner
An Obsidian feature called "Daily Note" creates a named entry from a template each morning. I have it prompt me to enter my tasks, link to my habit tracking and setup a section for free writing as well. Below is the template.

```
Time Start:
Time End:
Total:

#### Tasks
* [ ] Fill out  [[May Habits]] // hyperlink to a habit chart
* [ ] 

#### Free Writing (500 words)
```

Combined with an auto-launch from my desktop, it's easy to start mornings off with something active for my mind. The free-writing section works great for sorting through befuddled thoughts. Often time distraction follows from disorientation, which this technique mitigates well.
#### Organizing Scrolling Alternatives
The internet is full of so many Gems. I don't believe my true value preferences assign scrolling LinkedIn over an interesting blog on programming. The reason my observed behaviour differs is, I believe, primarily due to an accessibility problem. Browser bookmark folders work great for accumulating good blogs. Every time you find a cool blog, just add it in!
